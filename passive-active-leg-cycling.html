Movement explained / Lower-limb cycling

Movement starts with understanding.

Passive and active leg cycling describe different ways of moving the pedals. Here is what they mean—and what to ask when evaluating a wheelchair-integrated device.

Educational resource · LegMaker is an investigational device.

Two modes. Different roles.

01 / Passive

Motor-driven movement

A motor drives the pedals. The user's legs may move through the pedal cycle without supplying the driving force. Fit, positioning, comfort and supervision still require careful evaluation.

02 / Active

User-driven movement

The user provides the effort that turns the pedals, possibly against adjustable resistance. Suitability depends on individual ability, equipment setup and professional guidance.

What about assisted cycling?

Assisted cycling generally refers to equipment supporting part of the effort while a user contributes movement. Implementation differs across devices. Assisted and adaptive LegMaker functions are development concepts and should not be assumed available in a released product.

Five evaluation questions

  1. Is the device compatible with the wheelchair's width, frame and seating position?
  2. How are feet and lower limbs supported, and what happens if movement is obstructed?
  3. Who determines appropriate session duration, pace and supervision?
  4. Which controls and exercise modes are functional on the exact prototype being demonstrated?
  5. What testing, regulatory status and evidence support the intended use?
